# Utility, drivers, blockers, and trends

Direction and timing of change follow what is useful to someone who must pay, switch, comply, or give up a habit. Trends are descriptions of the recent past. They do not move themselves.

If the user wants a full filter after this, continue with [../forecast-filter/SKILL.md](../forecast-filter/SKILL.md).

## Utility first

Ask who gets a better job-to-be-done, at what cost, compared with the current workaround.

- If the new thing is slightly nicer and much harder, expect delay.
- If it removes a hated constraint (place, time, price, stigma) expect pull, even if the artifact is ugly.
- Producer utility (we can ship it) is not user utility (they will rearrange a Tuesday for it).

Picture phones vs pocket phones is the teaching contrast: a dazzling extra vs a blunt improvement in a job people already did.

## Drivers vs projected trends

A trend line without forces is a ruler laid on a chart.

**Drivers / enablers** to name: falling cost, new infrastructure, regulation that opens a door, status, complementary products, a powerful sponsor.

**Blockers / friction** to name with equal seriousness: switching cost, habit, incumbents, complementary gaps, stigma, law, morals, physical plant, “good enough” incumbents.

If blockers are absent, the author is in love with the slope.

## Trend-forecast failure modes

- Extending a growth rate past saturation or past a capacity cap.
- Treating a short boom as a law of nature.
- Ignoring substitution (the oil-by-2000 class of error: stocks without prices, efficiency, or new supply).
- Confusing category growth with a specific vendor’s destiny.
- Assuming change as the default. Stability is common. The hump that change must climb is large.
- Mixing product-ready dates with habit-ready dates. People change slower than SKUs.

## Extra questions

- Powerful backers and powerful veto players — named, not vibed?
- Whose side is the law on today, and what would have to happen for that to flip?
- Does the claim collide with culture or morals in a way money does not dissolve?
- Is the author in love with the technology?
- What is expected **not** to change? If “nothing is stable,” the model is theater.

## Output

```markdown
## Utility
**Job to be done:** …
**Who pays / switches / complies:** …
**Better than the current workaround?** yes | no | unknown — …

## Forces
**Drivers:** …
**Blockers:** …
**Law / norms / incumbents:** …
**Trend or mechanism?** trend-only | mechanism named

## Timing
**Product clock vs habit clock:** …
**Hump of change treated as:** real | rounding error
**What is assumed to stay put:** …

## Use
Adoption fragment you can keep. Trend fragment you should not extra-polate.
Leading indicators that would show blockers giving way.
```

## Traps

- “If we build it they will come.”
- “The growth rate continues.”
- “Incumbents are too slow” as a substitute for a switching-cost analysis.
- Dating the future by the author’s enthusiasm.

A forecast that cannot explain why a busy person would change their routine is not a forecast of uptake. It is a description of a prototype.
