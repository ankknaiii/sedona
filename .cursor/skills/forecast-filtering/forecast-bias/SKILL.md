# Forecast bias and mental models

Data does not interpret itself. Between the world and the prediction sit perceptual filters, incentives, and a model of how things work. Those can be ordinary (everyone has a beat) or steered (someone is selling). Both distort. Only the second is usually denied.

If the user wants a full filter after this, continue with [../forecast-filter/SKILL.md](../forecast-filter/SKILL.md).

## Two layers

**Interpretation bias.** What the author wants, fears, or is paid to conclude. Includes reputation risk: what do they lose if they are wrong?

**Mental-model lock.** What the author cannot easily see: professional training, national mood, the official future of their industry, the story that would make their last decade of work look wasted.

Name both. Do not treat “biased” as a synonym for “wrong.” A biased forecast can still be locally true. A sincere forecast can still be trapped in a dead paradigm.

## Bias checks

- **Natural vs intentional.** Domain habit (engineers over-weight technology; finance over-weights prices) vs a directed outcome (raise, elect, exonerate).
- **Skin in the game.** Reputation, book, fund, product, identity. “Nothing to lose by being vivid” is a tell.
- **Sponsored context.** Who paid, and would the piece have been published if the conclusion flipped?
- **Self-interest.** The author’s firm is the implied vendor, regulator, or hero.
- **Ideology and idealism.** The future is required to vindicate a moral picture.
- **Single-issue future.** One variable (climate, AI, China, fertility, oil) is asked to run the whole world.
- **Editorial vacuum.** No skeptical editor, red team, or competing brief.

## Zeitgeist and groupthink

- Could this have been generated from this year’s mood board?
- Bandwagon: fashionable object + recycled certainty.
- Expert theater: titles and panels in place of a mechanism.
- Official future: the version the industry already put in its strategy deck. Stretch thinking is the ability to put that future at risk.

## Assumptions

Ask, in this order:

1. Are assumptions written down?
2. Does the author know they are assumptions (not “how the world is”)?
3. Would they entertain a replacement set?
4. Do the assumptions match incentives and constraints you can actually observe?

Unstated assumptions are a worldview with a date stuck on.

## Output

```markdown
## Bias
**Natural slant:** …
**Steered slant:** none | describe
**Sponsor / self-interest / ideology:** …
**Skin in the game if wrong:** …

## Model lock
**Official future this protects:** …
**Assumptions (stated / implied):** …
**What the frame cannot see:** …
**Zeitgeist / bandwagon:** yes | no — …

## Use
What remains after you subtract the author’s needs and era-mood.
What must be independently re-derived before it enters planning.
```

## Traps

- Calling something unbiased because it uses charts.
- Calling something biased because you dislike the conclusion. Show the mechanism of distortion.
- Treating “a famous expert said so” as horizon scanning.
- Correcting spin by introducing the opposite spin. Filter; do not invert.

The goal is not purity. It is knowing whose eyes you are looking through, and whether those eyes can admit a different future.
