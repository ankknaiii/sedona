# Forecast filter hurdles

Apply these as tests, not as a reading list. Mark each `pass`, `fail`, or `unknown`. A fail removes or downgrades the parts that depend on that hurdle. It does not require binning the whole forecast.

## Purpose

- Is the purpose stated, or must the reader infer it from tone and venue?
- Is the piece trying to help the reader **align** with a future they do not control, or trying to **influence** a future into being?
- Is it broadcast widely, and does it tell the audience to do something in the world (buy, vote, fund, fear, adopt)?
- Does it live on extremes (collapse, miracle, “everything changes”) more than on a base case?

Influencing forecasts can still be useful as signals of someone’s campaign. They are not evidence that the campaign will work.

## Specificity

- Predictive (this will happen) or speculative (these futures are in play)?
- Too much certainty for the openness of the system?
- So much hedging that no decision can attach?
- Pace and timing named (year, range, leading indicator) or left as “soon”?

A useful forecast is explicit about mode. Point forecasts need dates and magnitudes. Exploratory work needs plural futures and what would distinguish them.

## Information quality

- How much base data is there, and is it fit for this claim (sample, definition, geography, time window)?
- Is it current enough that the mechanism has not already moved?
- Primary collection vs recycled secondary figures?
- Are quoted numbers observations, or earlier projections wearing a fact’s clothing?

Treat a projection inside the evidence pile as a claim, not as a measurement.

## Interpretation and bias

- Is the slant the ordinary kind (training, discipline, available data) or a steered kind (sale, ideology, identity)?
- Reputation: what does the author or house lose if this is wrong?
- Sponsored work, obvious self-interest, or a single-issue future that cannot admit tradeoffs?
- Did anyone with a different brief edit this, or did it go straight from advocate to audience?

## Methods and models

- Are methods named at a level a skeptical reader could replay?
- Is complexity, secrecy, or “proprietary model” used as a substitute for disclosure?
- Is novelty (“new and improved,” “AI-powered,” “first of its kind”) offered as a reason to believe?

Undisclosed method → cap confidence at `low`, whatever the prose swagger.

## Quantitative limits

- Are the quantities in a domain where counting and extrapolation are the right tools (stable categories, short horizon, closed system)?
- Is software producing the appearance of thought while the assumptions stay unexamined?

Numbers are allowed. Unexamined numbers are not an upgrade over a plain opinion.

## Managing complexity

- Does the world in the forecast stay still except for one celebrated variable?
- Are feedback loops named (adoption changing price, regulation reacting, competitors copying)?
- Are accelerators and derailers named (triggers, bottlenecks, tipping points)?
- Is exponential language doing work that a saturating S-curve should be doing?

## Assumptions and paradigm lock

- Was the horizon scanned for things outside the author’s usual beat?
- Are assumptions written down? Can the author entertain replacements?
- Do the assumptions survive contact with current incentives, law, and user behavior?

If assumptions are invisible, the forecast is a worldview with a date attached.

## Zeitgeist and groupthink

- Could this piece have been written by the mood of the year rather than by the evidence?
- Bandwagon: is the claim riding a fashionable technology, enemy, or statistic?
- “Experts agree” used as a substitute for a mechanism?
- Does the piece ever leave the official future (the one the industry already sold itself)?

## Drivers and blockers

- Are forces that **push** the outcome named, or is a trend line just extended?
- Are forces that **resist** named with comparable seriousness (cost, habit, incumbents, infrastructure, stigma)?
- Utility: who actually wants this enough to change behavior, and who is asked to absorb the pain?
- Powerful backers and powerful veto players?
- Collision with law, morals, or culture?
- Technology crush: is the author in love with the artifact more than with the job it does for a user?
- Timing errors: product-ready confused with habit-ready; the “hump” of change treated as a rounding error?
- What is assumed to stay the same? What has historically not changed on this clock?

## Reader utility (the last hurdle)

Even a high-quality forecast can be irrelevant.

- Does any surviving fragment change a decision this institution can actually take?
- If yes: what preparation, option, or bet follows?
- If no: file it as ambient chatter and stop spending attention.
