# Worked dissections

Use these as pattern templates, not as canned answers. When the user brings a new forecast, copy the verdict shape from the parent skill, not the conclusions below.

## 1. Picture phones missed; cell phones hit

**Claims (compressed):** a mass market for seeing the other caller; a mass market for carrying a phone.

| Gate | Picture phone | Cell phone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intent | Mixed: vendor-aligning plus a campaign to make the category real | Closer to aligning with a job people already paid to do (reach someone now) |
| Utility | Seeing the face was a nice extra, not the reason to pick up the phone | Mobility was the job. The device removed a location constraint people already hated |
| Blockers | Cost, bandwidth, social awkwardness, no network effects at launch | Regulation, infrastructure, and price were real — and were being paid down because utility was obvious |
| Complexity | Assumed video would ride along once voice existed | Network build-out, handset cost, and social norms had to move together; they did, slowly |
| Zeitgeist | Tech demonstration dressed as demand | Looked incremental, even ugly, while solving a blunt problem |

**Keep from the miss:** a working demo is not a market. Ask “what job is better than the current habit?” before “what can the lab do?”

**Keep from the hit:** forecasts that name a painful constraint and a paying workaround deserve attention even if the artifact looks crude.

**Error class:** technology-push without user utility; confusing “people will love this capability” with “people will change their call.”

## 2. Oil gone by 2000 missed; hybrids and alt-fuel uptake were directionally righter

**Claims (compressed):** reserves run out on a calendar date; mixed powertrains and non-oil fuels gain share.

| Gate | Exhaustion-by-date | Hybrids / alt fuels |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Specificity | Point date, high certainty, one variable (stock of oil) | Direction and mechanism (efficiency, policy, price, urban use) more than a single year |
| Information | Stocks treated as static; prices, substitution, and new extraction underweighted | Observed cost curves, regulation, and fleet turnover used as clocks |
| Complexity | Closed tank. No feedback from price to supply, efficiency, or geopolitics | Price, policy, OEM strategy, and driver utility interact |
| Drivers / blockers | Scarcity as destiny | Fuel cost, tax credits, range anxiety, charging/fueling friction, status |
| Quant limits | A depletion identity asked to do a systems job | Quant used for shares, costs, and lags — not for a prophetic date |

**Keep from the miss:** a physical limit can be real and still be a terrible calendar. Watch price, substitution, and behavior before you watch the “run-out” year.

**Keep from the hit:** forecasts that treat adoption as a contest among utilities (cheaper kilometer, less guilt, access to a city center) beat forecasts that treat a resource as a countdown clock.

**Error class:** single-issue resource fatalism; trend of “using more” projected without blockers and substitutes.

## 3. Breathless “latest new thing” article

Typical shape: one dazzling prototype, one large TAM, one quote from a founder, one chart that goes up and to the right, no friction.

Run every gate; these usually fail first:

- **Intent:** influencing (raise, sell, recruit).
- **Information:** demo + analog market size, not observed usage.
- **Utility:** assumed. Habit change treated as instant.
- **Blockers:** absent or mocked.
- **Method:** unnamed; novelty of the stack offered as proof.
- **Zeitgeist:** this week’s cluster (whatever suffix is fashionable).

**Default verdict:** `note` at most. Keep only a crisp statement of the job the artifact might do. Ignore TAM, dates, and “will replace X.” Re-open the file when there is evidence of paying users crossing a habit hump.

## 4. Mixed forecast you should not bin

A consultancy says: “Category Y will be 3× by 2031; incumbents who wait will be irrelevant; here are four no-regret moves.”

Dissect, do not swallow or spit.

- **Discount:** the 3× and the “irrelevant” threat (influencing, unspecified method, no blockers, official-future swagger).
- **Keep:** any no-regret move that survives without the 3× (interoperable data, cheaper switching, a capability that helps even if Y stays flat).
- **Prepare, do not decide:** watch a leading indicator the piece actually named (or that you add): price, attach rate, regulation date, switching cost.
- **Use:** `prepare` with `low`–`medium` confidence. The number is not the asset. The option set might be.

## Agent habit

When a user pastes a forecast, resist scoring it as true/false. Score it as **keep / discount / decide-or-not**, and name the error class if it fails. That is the whole method.
